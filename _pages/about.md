I am an applied mathematics PhD candidate in the [Department of Mathematics & Statistics](https://math.unm.edu) at the [University of New Mexico](https://www.unm.edu). My research lies at the intersection of mathematical biology, infectious disease epidemiology, and applied dynamical systems, with a particular focus on understanding how immune responses influence the transmission and long-term dynamics of infectious diseases.

My research focuses on understanding the complex dynamics of infectious diseases through mathematical modeling and computational analysis. I am particularly interested in how immune memory, waning immunity, and interactions among pathogen strains influence disease transmission and epidemic patterns. My work involves developing and analyzing differential equation models that incorporate antibody dynamics, cross-protective immunity, and other immune-mediated mechanisms. Through mathematical analysis and numerical simulations, I aim to gain deeper insights into infectious disease dynamics and contribute to a better understanding of the biological processes underlying disease spread and persistence.

I earned an M.S. in Applied Mathematics from [University of New Mexico](https://www.unm.edu) and both an M.S. and a B.Sc. in Mathematics from [Jahangirnagar University](https://juniv.edu), Dhaka, Bangladesh.  My academic journey has strengthened my appreciation for the role of mathematics in understanding real-world phenomena and has motivated me to pursue research that bridges mathematical theory and practical applications.

Alongside my research, teaching and academic mentorship are central to my professional identity. I have several years of university-level teaching experience in both the United States and Bangladesh. I have taught courses including Calculus, Applied Ordinary Differential Equations, College Algebra, Elementary Statistics, Linear Algebra, and Numerical Analysis. My teaching philosophy emphasizes conceptual understanding, active learning, and the development of mathematical reasoning and problem-solving skills. I strive to create an engaging, supportive, and inclusive classroom environment where students from diverse academic and cultural backgrounds feel encouraged to participate, ask questions, and build confidence in mathematics.

I am also passionate about fostering an equitable and inclusive academic community. I believe that mathematics becomes more meaningful and accessible when students are supported through thoughtful instruction, collaboration, and opportunities for intellectual growth.

Beyond research and teaching, I enjoy traveling, exploring new ideas, connecting with people from different backgrounds, and discovering experiences that broaden my personal and academic perspectives.
